Video Game:

Rick & Morty - Big Plumbusiness

Rick and Morty - Big Plumbusiness is a real-time logistics puzzle game set in the narrative of the Rick and Morty TV series.

Summary: As a player, you take control over various supply and production chains that have to be fine-tuned to reach predetermined quotas while battling the concurrently rising pollution of the planets. Almost every production creates pollution while generating income that can be invested in environmental projects. Players have to synchronize production amounts to reduce the waste of resources and stay ahead of pollution.
It still has some rough edges, but vibrant colours, smooth controls and a homely concept mixed with funny dialogues make for an entertaining and captivating game experience.
Language: English
Technology: Unity, C#, Photoshop
Contributor(s): Pitt Michelmann

Video Game:

Pacmansion

Pacmansion is a reinvention of the video game classic Pacman from the first-person perspective.

Summary: In this version, Pacman has to find his way through the maze from an ego perspective, giving it a novel, dark and oppressive atmosphere. Players have to continuously collect coins while evading the ghosts to keep the health bar from running out. Ghosts will follow the player on sight but with the right power-up the hunters might become the hunted.
Made in Unreal Engine 4 with flashy light effects and a nifty soundtrack.

APP:

SOOL - Soundtrack Of Our Lives

SOOL is a location based social music discovery application for Android devices

Summary: The main feature of SOOL is synchronous Spotify playback with collocated strangers. Users can share their currently playing song in a certain radius so that nearby strangers that also have the app installed can listen with them.
Modern music listening practices often lead to isolating behaviour when in public. SOOL is meant to generate a feeling of connectedness and curiosity among strangers and even lead to technology-mediated encounters.
SOOL also provides a novel and fresh source for music recommendations.
In a user study, I could verify its potential for music discovery and fostering curiosity towards strangers. For a more detailed explanation and analysis, feel free to look into my master thesis further down the site.

Language: English
Technology: React Native, Javascript, MongoDB, OAuth 2.0, Android Studio
Contributor(s): Pitt Michelmann

Bachelor Thesis:

VRestionnaires: Usability Analysis of In-VR-Questionnaires

Abstract: Standardized questionnaires are essential tools for Virtual Reality (VR) researchers to evaluate their developed systems. An analysis of state-of-the-art studies and an online questionnaire among 67 VR researchers showed a lack of consistent standards regarding the presentation of questionnaires and that this domain is vastly under-researched. Based on these findings, we developed a questionnaire generation tool to create and embed integrated VR questionnaires. In-VR questionnaires are meant to simplify VR studies' work and data flow. Furthermore, integrated VR questionnaires can potentially influence the results and findings of the VR studies to which they are applied. We implemented design paradigms for interaction and placement commonly found in literature and current studies. We conducted an expert evaluation with 10 participants to compare the conditions and evaluate the overall usability of the questionnaires. Our findings show that the different conditions highly influence usability. We further rate the aptitude of the analyzed characteristics for use in In-VR questionnaires.

Language: German
Contributor(s): Pitt Michelmann

Research Paper:

Examining Design Choices of Questionnaires in VR User Studies

Abstract: Questionnaires are among the most common research tools in virtual reality (VR) user studies. Transitioning from virtuality to reality for giving self-reports on VR experiences can lead to systematic biases. VR allows to embed questionnaires into the virtual environment which may ease participation and avoid biases. To provide a cohesive picture of methods and design choices for questionnaires in VR (inVRQ), we discuss 15 inVRQ studies from the literature and present a survey with 67 VR experts from academia and industry. Based on the outcomes, we conducted two user studies in which we tested different presentation and interaction methods of inVRQs and evaluated the usability and practicality of our design. We observed comparable completion times between inVRQs and questionnaires outside VR (nonVRQs) with higher enjoyment but lower usability for inVRQs. These findings advocate the application of inVRQs and provide an overview of methods and considerations that lay the groundwork for inVRQ design.

Language: English
Contributor(s): Dmitry Alexandrovsky, Susanne Putze, Michael Bonfert, Sebastian Höffner, Pitt Michelmann, Dirk Wenig, Rainer Malaka, Jan David Smeddinck

Research Paper:

Conversational User Interfaces to support Astronauts in Extraterrestrial Habitats

Abstract: Long-term space missions are challenging and demanding for astronauts. Confined spaces and long-duration sensory deprivation may cause psychological problems for the astronauts. In this paper, we envision how extraterrestrial habitats (e.g., a habitat on the Moon or Mars) can maintain the well-being of the crews by augmenting the astronauts. In particular, we report on the design, implementation, and evaluation of conversational user interfaces (CUIs) for extraterrestrial habitats. The goal of such CUIs is to support scientists during their daily and scientific routines on their missions within the extraterrestrial habitat and provide emotional support. During a week-long so-called analog mission with four scientists using a Wizard of Oz prototype, we derived design guidelines for such CUIs. Successively, based on the derived guidelines, we present the implementation and evaluation of two CUIs named CASSIOPEIA and PEGASUS.

Language: English
Contributor(s): Johannes Schöning, Ana Rita Gonçalves Freitas, Alexander Schülke, Simon Glaser, Pitt Michelmann, Thanh Nguyen Chi, Lisa Schröder, Zahra Fadavi, Zahra Fadavi, Jette Ternieten, Akash Trivedi, Jana Wahls, Warda Masood, Christiane Heinicke

Master Thesis:

Sociability of Mobile Listening Technologies in Public Urban Surroundings

Abstract: This thesis analyzes the social aspects of modern mobile music consumption in public spaces. We researched the changes in social behavior brought by advancing technologies to identify social challenges and limitations of modern consumption patterns. We found factors in modern mobile music consumption limiting social interactions and exchange and serendipitous music discovery. This thesis aims to devise universal best practices and design paradigms for creating applications focused on improving mobile listening experiences and engaging the isolating effects of mobile listening practices.
We analyzed studies on location-based applications, music discovery and urban computing and collected their design choices and findings. As an exemplary implementation, we derived our own concept and prototype application, Soundtrack Of Our Lives (SOOL). SOOL enables the real-time sharing of music playback with collocated strangers in urban places using mobile phones and Spotify. We conducted a user study using our prototype and discuss its potential to overcome public social isolation and instead create a sense of social cohesion. This study’s findings should help to create guidelines for future research in the field of location-based mobile music sharing in urban public environments.

Language: English
Contributor(s): Pitt Michelmann
